High-Alert Safety Rules — All in One Place

The hardest rules from every week, condensed. Each week's own Big Picture page has the full list.

WeekThe Hard Rules
Week 1Telemetry can't diagnose: get a 12-lead within 10 minutes. One nitro without relief at home: call 911 and chew an aspirin; check the BP before nitro. After a femoral cath: leg straight, head of bed below 30°, watch for bleeding. Tamponade: muffled heart sounds, JVD, pulsus paradoxus. Mechanical valve: lifetime anticoagulation.
Week 2Retinal detachment and acute angle-closure glaucoma are emergencies; no dilating drops with closed-angle glaucoma; miotic before darkening the room. A patient who can't urinate is an emergency; CBI to light pink. Three reportable STDs; treat partners.
Week 3A monitor change: check the patient first. Symptomatic bradycardia: atropine, then pacing. A-fib over 48 hours: anticoagulate and TEE before cardioversion. V-fib: CPR and defibrillate with the sync off. Digoxin: apical pulse a full minute, hold below 60. No SCDs once a VTE is present.
Week 4Never give insulin without the current glucose. The rule of 15; unresponsive: glucagon or IV dextrose. Hypertonic saline slowly. Never stop steroids abruptly; the 3 x 3 rule. Check behind the neck after thyroidectomy; watch for hypocalcemia.
Week 5Pain unrelieved by medication is the first neurovascular sign; compartment syndrome is an emergency. Traction weights hang free. VTE is the top joint replacement risk; know the hip precautions by approach. Acetaminophen 3 g limit. Alendronate upright 30 minutes.
Week 6No metal in MRI; hold stimulants before an EEG. Seizures: airway, nothing in the mouth, no restraints; status epilepticus at 5 minutes gets a benzodiazepine. GBS: watch the gag reflex and breathing. Stroke: time of onset, no tPA with blood on the CT, NPO until the swallow evaluation, frequent NIH Stroke Scale checks.
