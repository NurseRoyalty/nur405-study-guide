Thyroid Disorders: Hyperthyroidism & Hypothyroidism

Built from the Week 4 thyroid disorders voiceover. A happy thyroid is a euthyroid state. The extremes, thyroid storm and myxedema coma, are covered in pathopharm with the endocrine emergencies. "Think metabolism when I think thyroid."

The Feedback Loop and Key Terms

The anterior pituitary secretes TSH, which triggers the thyroid to secrete T3 and T4; circulating T3 and T4 tell the pituitary to shut off TSH. "You better not get confused about whether the arrow goes up or down."

FeatureHyperthyroidismHypothyroidism
T3 and T4↑ Too much circulating↓ Low
TSH↓ The pituitary tries to shut the thyroid off, but it isn't working↑ High: trying to get the "lazy thyroid" to work
Most common causeGraves' disease (autoimmune), three-fourths of the time; also toxic multinodular goiterHashimoto's thyroiditis (autoimmune)
The extremeThyroid storm (acute thyrotoxicosis, thyrotoxic crisis)Myxedema coma

Goiter

  • Simply a large thyroid gland, often visible in the neck, from an overactive or underactive thyroid.
  • Toxic goiter: large from hyperthyroidism. Nontoxic goiter: large with a normal thyroid level.

Iodine

  • Needed to synthesize thyroid hormones; only the thyroid can take it up.
  • Deficiency is a worldwide problem but not so much in the US, because iodized salt is the norm (buy iodized unless you react to iodine).

Temperature—Cold intolerance
Unique findingsBruit over the thyroid with the bell (increased vascularity); exophthalmos (fat and edema push the eyeballs out; severe, they may not close)Possibly a goiter
Thyroid storm (acute thyrotoxicosis) is hyperthyroidism to the extreme: rare, and death is rare when treated early. It results from stressors in someone with pre-existing hyperthyroidism, for example the manipulation of the gland during a thyroidectomy. All the hyperthyroid symptoms become prominent and severe.

Managing Hyperthyroidism

TreatmentKey Points
MethimazoleDecreases thyroid activity, but slowly: 1 to 2 weeks to kick in, 4 to 8 weeks for good results.
Beta blockers (PO)Treat the symptoms in the meantime, until the other therapies take effect.
Iodine therapy (SSKI, Lugol's solution)Used to prepare for a thyroidectomy: decreases the gland's vascularity and the risk of bleeding after surgery.
Radioactive iodine (I-131)The treatment of choice if not pregnant; 60% of Graves' patients receive it. Deliberately kills off the overactive thyroid (ablation) over 6 to 18 weeks, so lifelong thyroid replacement follows. An unsealed radiation source: excretions are radioactive. The radioactivity is gone in a few days; minimize direct contact for 2 to 3 days, double flush the toilet, keep things separate.
Subtotal thyroidectomyRemoves up to 90% of the gland, for those who don't respond to anything else; removing too much can create hypothyroidism. Endoscopic thyroidectomy also exists.

After a Thyroidectomy

A risky surgery, so close to the airway. Watch for hemorrhage (the thyroid is very vascular) and tracheal compression. Always look behind the neck: blood follows gravity, so a clean front dressing doesn't rule out bleeding. Watch the vital signs.
  • Hoarseness is expected for 3 to 4 days.
  • Semi-Fowler's; avoid flexion or tension on the suture lines.
  • If the parathyroids are removed: hypocalcemia (tingling, muscle twitching, apprehension; Trousseau's carpopedal spasm with a BP cuff, Chvostek's cheek twitch when tapped).

Managing Hypothyroidism

  • Levothyroxine (thyroid replacement); monitor thyroid levels and adjust as needed.
  • Labs: T3 and T4 low, TSH high. Antibodies present = autoimmune (Hashimoto's).
  • Nutrition to promote weight loss if they gained weight from the hypothyroidism.
Myxedema coma is severe hypothyroidism and a medical emergency: mental status changes, possibly comatose. Treated with IV thyroid medication and steroids.
