Endocrine Labs & Dx

Know for the exam

  • ★ Endocrine questions turn on the arrows: if you only half understand the content, you'll pick the up arrow when it should be down. Learn which values rise and fall.
  • Negative feedback: when a hormone level rises, the gland is told to stop producing it, and vice versa.
  • ADH moves pure water, not potassium: ADH problems change sodium and osmolality, never potassium ("that's going to trip you up").
  • Aldosterone is the one that moves potassium: aldosterone up, sodium up and potassium down.

ADH Disorders

Know for the exam

  • ★ "Really important that you understand these arrows": SIADH dilutes (sodium and serum osmolality down); DI concentrates (sodium and osmolality up).
  • SIADH: fluid volume excess; restrict fluids (about 1 L a day), daily weights, I&O, mental status checks.
  • Severe hyponatremia (below about 110 to 115) brings confusion and lethargy; hypertonic saline is given slowly (too fast can be fatal).
  • DI: polyuria of very dilute urine, thirst; neurogenic after head trauma (abrupt, often self-limiting) or nephrogenic (lithium).
  • On desmopressin: report rising urine volume with falling specific gravity (the DI is worsening).

Adrenal Disorders

Know for the exam

  • Cushing's syndrome is usually from long-term oral steroids: moon face, buffalo hump, thin skin and bruising, high glucose, low potassium.
  • Cushing's priorities: risk for infection (immunosuppression), skin integrity, glucose; diet up protein and potassium, down calories and sodium.
  • Steroids for another condition are never stopped abruptly: decrease gradually, reduce the dose or go every other day, provider supervised.
  • Addison's: not enough cortisol and aldosterone: low sodium, high potassium, low BP, hypoglycemia, fatigue, bronze skin.
  • ★ "Teaching is critical": hydrocortisone two-thirds in the morning, one-third in the late afternoon; never stop abruptly; the 3 x 3 rule when stressed; an emergency supply and a medical alert bracelet.
  • Addisonian crisis (a big stressor without the supplement): IV cortisol, saline and dextrose; trend vitals; calm environment.
  • Pheochromocytoma: intractable hypertension plus palpitations, headache and sweating; serum catecholamines; remove the tumor.

Not Tested

  • The medications for Cushing's ("need not know for this particular course").

Thyroid Disorders

Know for the exam

  • ★ "You better not get confused about whether the arrow goes up or down": hyperthyroid has a low TSH; hypothyroid has a high TSH.
  • Hyperthyroid (Graves' three-fourths of the time) revs everything up; hypothyroid (Hashimoto's) slows everything down.
  • Methimazole is slow (weeks), so beta blockers treat the symptoms meanwhile. SSKI or Lugol's before thyroidectomy reduces vascularity and bleeding.
  • Radioactive iodine: minimize contact for 2 to 3 days, double flush; lifelong replacement follows.
  • After thyroidectomy: check behind the neck for bleeding, watch the airway, semi-Fowler's; hoarseness for 3 to 4 days is expected.
  • Tingling, twitching, Trousseau's and Chvostek's signs after surgery mean hypocalcemia (parathyroids).
  • Thyroid storm is a stressor in pre-existing hyperthyroidism; myxedema coma is an emergency treated with IV thyroid medication and steroids.

DM: Patho, Pharm & Nursing Care

Know for the exam

  • ★ Insulin is high alert: never give it without knowing the current blood glucose ("always, always, always"); check the diet order and intake.
  • ★ Know onset, peak and duration for the insulin types: rapid-acting starts in 5 to 10 minutes; basal-bolus is glargine at bedtime plus rapid or regular before meals.
  • ★ Sick-day management was pulled straight from patho ("that's how important we think this is"): keep taking meds, check sugar every 2 to 4 hours, fluids, carbs, call the provider.
  • ★ "Please do know" the rule of 15: 15 g simple carbohydrate (4 to 6 oz juice or regular soda, 3 glucose tabs, 1 tablespoon honey), recheck in 15 minutes.
  • Unresponsive or can't swallow: IM glucagon or IV dextrose. Unresponsive patient: after the airway, check the blood sugar.
  • ★ Expect a select-all question on foot care teaching (what to do and what not to do).
  • A1C of 6.5% or more suggests diabetes; a fasting glucose over 126 is repeated before diagnosing.
  • Don't exercise below 80 or above 250. Long-time diabetics often know their bodies: listen when they say their sugar is dropping.
  • NPO with scheduled insulin follows hospital policy; pumps are deactivated in acute care.

Not Tested

  • Inhaled insulin.
  • Glucose values in mmol/L (use mg/dL).
  • The hospital diet order names.
  • Specific side effects of the oral diabetes drugs (know the classes).
