Eye Disorders

Know for the exam

  • ★ "You will be tested on [the eye] in HESI as well as the NCLEX": commit these disorders to memory even though they're rarely why a med-surg patient is admitted.
  • Normal IOP is 10 to 21 mmHg; Goldmann tonometry is the gold standard; test IOP for anyone over 40.
  • MyDriatic drops Dilate; miotic drops constrict. Never give dilating drops with closed-angle glaucoma.
  • Cataracts: constant fuzzy vision with glare. After eye surgery, avoid anything that raises IOP (bending at the waist, straining, lifting, coughing).
  • Retinal detachment is urgent: sudden floaters or flashes, then a painless curtain.
  • ★ A scleral buckle (the third detached-retina procedure, after laser and cryopexy) was once on the HESI.
  • AMD takes central vision; glaucoma takes peripheral vision (often unnoticed).
  • Acute angle-closure glaucoma is an emergency: treat pain and nausea aggressively, and give the miotic before darkening the room.

Not Tested

  • The scleral buckle video (optional).
  • Glaucoma medication specifics beyond what is on the slide.

Prostate & BPH

Know for the exam

  • ★ TURP is the gold standard surgery and the one the HESI and NCLEX most often ask about: "commit this one to memory."
  • ★ The three-way catheter after a TURP is "an important idea to understand": CBI is titrated to light pink urine.
  • True urine output = total drained minus the irrigation. A large amount of bright red blood may mean hemorrhage.
  • PSA is specific to the prostate, not to cancer; only TRUS with biopsy diagnoses cancer.
  • BPH: irritative and obstructive symptoms (nocturia first); avoid decongestants and antihistamines; being unable to urinate is an emergency.
  • BPH does not predispose to prostate cancer. Screening is a shared decision: average risk at 50 (45 for higher risk); not recommended over 75.

STDs

Know for the exam

  • Gonorrhea, chlamydia and syphilis (the three bacterial ones) are reportable to the health department.
  • ★ Epididymitis was once on the HESI: the duct behind the testes that carries sperm (a gonorrhea and chlamydia cue in men).
  • Treat partners (contacts from the prior 60 days), abstain at least 7 days after treatment, and retest.
  • Chlamydia and gonorrhea can cause PID; trichomoniasis can't. The male condom is the best protection; birth control and IUDs don't prevent STIs.
  • Herpes is lifelong (acyclovir); HSV-1 above the waist, HSV-2 below, most of the time.
  • Removing warts doesn't clear HPV. Syphilis is treated with penicillin G; test for HIV with syphilis.
