The Lectures' Own Order

Week 3 starts with the heart's electricity (normal, then sinus, atrial and ventricular rhythms), then the pump failing (heart failure), then the vessels: three arterial disorders and two venous ones.

StepWhere It LivesThe Common Thread
The Electricity and the StripTopic 2, Intro & Sinus DysrhythmiasSA node at 60 to 100, the waveform (P, PR, QRS, T), the 6-second strip, telemetry, and the key-features slide every other rhythm points back to. Sinus brady (atropine if symptomatic) and sinus tach (treat the cause).
Above the VentriclesTopic 3, Atrial DysrhythmiasPACs can lead to A-fib; A-fib and A-flutter: control the rate first, then the rhythm, and prevent clots. PSVT: vagal maneuvers, then adenosine. Unstable: synchronized cardioversion.
In the VentriclesTopic 4, Ventricular DysrhythmiasPVCs can lead to V-tach, which can lead to V-fib. Pulse or no pulse decides everything. V-fib: CPR, ACLS, defibrillate with the sync off.
The Pump and the ArteriesTopic 5, HF, PAD, AAA & Raynaud'sLeft failure backs up into the lungs, right failure into the body; BNP, daily weights, digoxin. PAD: claudication and the ABI. AAA: watch it or fix it before it ruptures. Raynaud's: vasospasm.
The VeinsTopic 6, CVI & VTEGetting blood back to the heart: edema, brown leathery skin, elevate the legs. VTE: stasis, damage, hypercoagulability; prevent it, and watch for PE.

The Five Topics at a Glance

#TopicOne-Line Takeaway
2Intro & Sinus DysrhythmiasPR 0.12 to 0.20, QRS skinny (0.10 or less), each tiny box 0.04, count the QRSs on a 6-second strip and multiply by 10. Something changes on the monitor: go check the patient. Symptomatic bradycardia: atropine 1 mg IV every 3 to 5 minutes (3 mg max), then transcutaneous pacing.
3Atrial DysrhythmiasNew PACs: check magnesium, calcium and potassium. A-fib: no P waves, irregularly irregular, the most common dysrhythmia, risk of stroke. A-flutter: sawtooth F waves. You need an R wave to cardiovert; A-fib over 48 hours needs anticoagulation and a TEE first. Adenosine: fast push, fast flush.
4Ventricular DysrhythmiasPVCs are early, wide and ugly; bigeminy means every other beat. Three in a row is V-tach (150 to 200). V-fib has zero cardiac output. Defibrillate at 200 up to 360 joules with the sync off; cardiovert at 50 to 200 with it on.
5HF, PAD, AAA & Raynaud'sCrackles and PND are left-sided; JVD, ascites and edema are right-sided. BNP is to heart failure as troponin is to MI. Hold digoxin for an apical rate below 60. PAD: no pulses, no hair, pale when raised, ABI below 0.9. AAA under 4 cm: watchful waiting. Raynaud's: warmth, avoid triggers, calcium channel blocker.
6CVI & VTECVI: edema, brawny leathery skin, painful ulcers above the medial malleolus; elevate and compress. VTE: unilateral swelling, warmth, tenderness; mobilize early, anticoagulate, and watch for a PE.

High-Alert Safety Rules — All in One Place

The danger callouts and safety statements from across the five pages, condensed. Click through to a topic for the full explanation.

TopicThe Hard Rules
Intro & SinusA monitor change: go check on the patient first. Symptomatic bradycardia: atropine 1 mg IV every 3 to 5 minutes (3 mg max), then transcutaneous pacing. Asymptomatic bradycardia: monitor, don't treat.
AtrialNew or increasing PACs: check electrolytes and tell the provider. Cardioversion needs an R wave and the sync on; clear the bed. A-fib over 48 hours: anticoagulate 4 to 6 weeks and TEE before cardioversion. Adenosine: rapid push and immediate flush; warn about the pause. Check creatinine before the newer anticoagulants.
VentricularIncreasing PVCs: tell the provider (V-tach risk). Check for a pulse. V-fib and pulseless V-tach: call a code, CPR, defibrillate with the sync off.
HF, PAD, AAA & Raynaud'sDigoxin: apical pulse for a full minute, hold below 60; watch potassium. ADHF: high Fowler's, legs dangling. After a PAD procedure: report loss of pulse, more pain or pallor; no knee flexion. After AAA repair: BP adequate but not too high, hourly urine output, distal perfusion.
CVI & VTENo SCDs once a VTE is present. Anticoagulation means bleeding risk. Watch for a pulmonary embolism.
