Neuro Labs & Diagnostics

Built from the Week 6 neuro labs and diagnostics voiceover. Most of these tests come back on the disease pages: the EEG with seizures, EMG and nerve conduction tests with myasthenia gravis, ALS and Guillain-Barré, evoked potentials and the lumbar puncture with MS, the carotid duplex with stroke. Neuro diagnosis is rarely a single blood draw; it leans on the history, the exam and these tests.

Electroencephalogram (EEG)

The test

  • Measures the brain's electrical activity: "the EEG is to the brain as the EKG is to the heart."
  • Assesses for the presence of different types of seizures.
  • Withhold stimulants for 8 hours beforehand (no activity from artificial stimulants).
  • Noninvasive and painless; at the bedside or in an EEG lab; usually 20 to 40 minutes.

The epilepsy monitoring unit

  • For patients with intractable seizures not helped by traditional therapies.
  • Their seizure medications are stopped before they come in, and they go on a continuous EEG.
  • The goal is for the patient to have a seizure there, so its location and severity can be recorded: like a cardiac EP study, deliberately triggering the problem in a controlled environment.

Lumbar Puncture (Spinal Tap)

A myelogram is an x-ray of the spinal cord after contrast is injected into the subarachnoid space. A lumbar puncture is different: the provider taps the spinal column to obtain cerebrospinal fluid.

Before and during

  • It's invasive: a consent form.
  • Have the patient empty the bladder (a smaller abdomen, more comfortable).
  • Position: lateral recumbent, curled in the fetal position, to open the space between the vertebrae. Some sit on a hard surface and lean over. The lecture also mentions a tilt table.

After

  • The patient may or may not need to lie flat for an hour. For years patients lay flat for hours to prevent a spinal headache, but current evidence doesn't show that longer bed rest means less headache. Follow the provider's orders.
  • Monitor for a spinal headache, caused by losing fluid from the spinal column.
Treating a spinal headache: start with bed rest and analgesics (possibly opioids; they can be severe). Next, a blood patch: blood drawn from the periphery is injected into the dura. IV caffeine has also been shown to help.

Which scan shows an organ's physiology (metabolism) rather than its anatomy?
A PET scan.
What does increased velocity on a carotid duplex suggest?
Stenosis (narrowing) of the carotid artery.
How long are stimulants withheld before an EEG?
8 hours.
Why are seizure meds stopped in the epilepsy monitoring unit?
So the patient has a seizure on continuous EEG and its location and severity can be recorded.
Which test measures nerve innervation and is used for ALS and MG?
EMG (electromyography).
Which two tests are specific to myasthenia gravis?
Repetitive nerve stimulation and single fiber EMG.
Which test uses a checkerboard and a red dot, and for which disease?
A visual evoked potential, for MS.
Myelogram vs lumbar puncture?
A myelogram is an x-ray after contrast; an LP taps the spine to obtain CSF.
What should the patient do right before a lumbar puncture?
Empty the bladder.
What position is used for a lumbar puncture?
Lateral recumbent, curled in the fetal position.
What causes a spinal headache?
Loss of fluid from the spinal column.
Three treatments for a spinal headache?
Bed rest and analgesics, a blood patch, IV caffeine.
