Ventricular Dysrhythmias: PVC, V-Tach & V-Fib

Built from the Week 3 ventricular dysrhythmias voiceover. These "can be scary": they are very life-threatening. ACLS is a course of its own; for the 405 exam the instructor will ask only what is on these slides.

Premature Ventricular Contraction (PVC)

  • A premature beat from an ectopic focus in the ventricles. It comes earlier than the QRS should and doesn't follow the normal rhythm.
  • "Premature and it's wide and it's ugly": a wide, distorted QRS that doesn't look like the others. It typically does not perfuse.
  • Causes: stimulants, electrolytes (always check them with dysrhythmias), hypoxia, fever, exercise, emotional stress, cardiovascular disease.
  • Treatment: find and fix the cause. Drugs for a tendency toward PVCs: beta blockers, lidocaine, amiodarone.
PatternWhat It Means
BigeminyA PVC every other beat. Since PVCs don't usually perfuse, only every other beat is perfusing.
TrigeminyA PVC every third beat.
QuadrigeminyA PVC every fourth beat.
Unifocal PVCsAll from the same irritable focus (they look alike).
Multifocal PVCsFrom more than one irritable focus (one goes up, another goes down). "Makes me raise my eyebrows even further."
PVCs increasing in frequency, or new-onset: watch closely and let the provider know. An isolated PVC can be benign (some patients always have a few), but a patient with a PVC every other beat may be about to convert to V-tach, a crisis.

Ventricular Tachycardia (V-Tach) and Ventricular Fibrillation (V-Fib)

FeatureV-TachV-Fib
DefinitionThree PVCs in a row is, in theory, V-tach. An ectopic focus takes over; the ventricle fires repeatedly with no atrial contraction. Sustained V-tach keeps going.The ventricles just quiver: irregular waveforms with no QRSs.
Rate and rhythm150 to 200; usually regular. No P wave; the PR interval isn't measurable.No measurable rate.
Cardiac outputA serious decrease: too fast for the ventricles to fill.Zero. A deadly rhythm.
CausesElectrolyte imbalances, heart failure, drug toxicity.Untreated V-tach slipping further (the further they slip, the less well treatment works).
The key questionDoes the patient have a pulse? A pulse means perfusion; no pulse means no perfusion, and the patient becomes symptomatic very quickly.
TreatmentACLS. With a pulse: an antidysrhythmic (beta blocker, calcium channel blocker, or amiodarone); check and replace electrolytes immediately. Pulseless V-tach: defibrillate.CPR, ACLS and defibrillation. Call a code: this is cardiac arrest.
You can live with A-fib. You cannot live with V-fib.

Cardioversion vs Defibrillation

Synchronized cardioversion (sedation, clearing the bed, A-fib over 48 hours) covered again here — review in Atrial Dysrhythmias if needed

FeatureSynchronized CardioversionDefibrillation
WhenMore elective; the rhythm has an R wave (A-fib, A-flutter, unstable PSVT).An emergency: pulseless V-tach and V-fib. No cardiac output.
PatientUsually awake; sedated if there is time.Unconscious.
Sync buttonON: the machine synchronizes the shock with the QRS.OFF. Left on, it would never fire, because it waits for a QRS and V-fib has none. Off, it fires whenever you push the button.
Energy50 to 200 joulesBegin at 200 joules, up to 360 joules. More joules than cardioversion.
AlsoMonitor and document the result.On the cardiac monitor; the nurse is calling a code.

Self-Test Flashcards

Click a card to flip it and check your answer.

How does a PVC look on the strip?
Early, wide and distorted ("wide and ugly").
What is bigeminy?
A PVC every other beat.
Multifocal PVCs come from how many foci?
More than one.
PVCs increasing in frequency raise concern for conversion to what?
V-tach.
How many PVCs in a row make V-tach?
Three.
What is the rate range of V-tach?
150 to 200.
What is the key question to ask in V-tach?
Does the patient have a pulse?
What is the cardiac output in V-fib?
Zero.
What is the treatment for V-fib?
CPR, ACLS and defibrillation.
Sync button on or off to defibrillate?
Off.
Why won't a synced shock fire in V-fib?
There are no QRSs to sync with.
What is the starting energy for defibrillation?
200 joules.
