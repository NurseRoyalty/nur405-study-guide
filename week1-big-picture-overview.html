The Lectures' Own Order

Week 1 runs from the tests, to the most common problem (blocked coronary arteries), to the three inflammatory, infective, and valvular conditions. The instructor introduced them as one set: "caring for the patient with inflammatory, infective and valvular heart disease."

StepWhere It LivesThe Common Thread
The Tools — How We Look at the HeartTopic 2, CV Labs & DiagnosticsSerum markers (troponin for MI, BNP for heart failure, CRP and ESR for inflammation), then non-invasive tests (12-lead, tele, Holter, stress test, echo), then invasive ones (TEE, EP study, heart cath). Every later topic leans on these.
The Blocked Artery — CAD to MITopic 3, CAD & Acute Coronary SyndromeA continuum: stable angina, unstable angina, NSTEMI, STEMI. Atherosclerosis causes almost all of it. Time is muscle: PCI within 90 minutes, thrombolytics within 30.
Outside the Heart — InflammationTopic 4, PericarditisThe pericardium is inflamed; the friction rub is the hallmark. Its danger is fluid: effusion, then tamponade.
Inside the Heart — InfectionTopic 5, Infective EndocarditisThe endocardium and valves are infected; vegetations break off as emboli. Its most common complication is heart failure.
The Doors — ValvesTopic 6, Valvular Heart DiseaseStenosis (won't open), regurgitation (won't close), prolapse (buckles). All of them end up looking like heart failure.

The Five Topics at a Glance

#TopicOne-Line Takeaway
2CV Labs & DiagnosticsTroponin is the most sensitive marker of myocardial damage (up in 2 to 3 hours, back in 10 to 14 days); a 12-lead can diagnose an MI but telemetry only monitors; only a left heart cath reaches the coronary arteries; contrast dye threatens the kidneys.
3CAD & Acute Coronary SyndromeStable angina is predictable and relieved by rest or nitro; unstable angina, NSTEMI, and STEMI are ACS. One nitro without relief at home means call 911 and chew an aspirin; always check BP before nitro. Dysrhythmia is the most common MI complication.
4PericarditisInflammation of the pericardium: positional pain, better leaning forward, worse on inspiration, not relieved by nitro; friction rub at the lower left sternal border; diffuse ST elevation; treated with NSAIDs. Watch for tamponade.
5Infective EndocarditisNeeds damaged epithelium plus a port of entry; flu-like at first; splinter hemorrhages, petechiae, Osler nodes, Janeway lesions, and Roth spots are the findings to memorize; three blood cultures; weeks of IV antibiotics.
6Valvular Heart DiseaseKnow your valves and landmarks; repair before replace; mechanical valves last longer but need lifetime anticoagulation and click, biologic valves need none but wear out sooner.

High-Alert Safety Rules — All in One Place

The danger callouts and safety statements from across the five pages, condensed. Click through to a topic for the full explanation.

TopicThe Hard Rules
CV Labs & DiagnosticsTelemetry cannot diagnose; get a 12-lead. After a cath: watch for bleeding and hematoma, hold pressure, bed rest with the leg straight and head of bed below 30 degrees after a femoral approach, check distal pulses, color, and sensation. Contrast dye: check BUN and creatinine, force fluids after. TEE: NPO until the gag reflex returns.
CAD & ACSOne nitro without relief at home: call 911 and chew an aspirin. Check BP before every nitro. Never combine nitro with ED drugs; never defibrillate over a nitro patch. 12-lead within 10 minutes of arrival. A rising heart rate after PCI can mean internal bleeding. No thrombolytics with a history of hemorrhagic stroke, recent abdominal surgery, active bleeding, or abnormal coagulation studies.
PericarditisCardiac tamponade is time-sensitive and life-threatening: muffled heart sounds, JVD, narrowed pulse pressure, pulsus paradoxus. Emergency pericardiocentesis.
Infective EndocarditisAcute endocarditis needs prompt, vigorous therapy. Prophylactic antibiotics before dental work and other listed procedures for high-risk patients. Vegetations can embolize: left side to organs and limbs, right side to the lungs.
Valvular Heart DiseaseMechanical valve: lifetime anticoagulation and INR monitoring; vitamin K is the antidote; consistent vitamin K intake; electric razor; no MRI; medical alert bracelet.
