High-Alert Safety Rules — All in One Place

The danger callouts and safety statements from across the four pages, condensed. Click through to a topic for the full explanation.

TopicThe Hard Rules
Neuro Labs & DxCT contrast: allergies, IV, kidney status. No metal in an MRI. Hold stimulants 8 hours before an EEG. LP: consent, empty the bladder, fetal position; follow orders on lying flat; watch for a spinal headache (bed rest, analgesics, blood patch, IV caffeine).
Parkinson's, MG & MSPD: give meds promptly, prevent falls and aspiration, avoid high-protein meals with levodopa. MG: time meds to peak at meals; know myasthenic (too little) vs cholinergic (too much) crisis, both can cause respiratory failure. MS: avoid heat; rest; exercise.
ALS, Seizures & GBSALS: NPO once aspiration risk begins; still assess pain. Seizures: airway first, nothing in the mouth, don't restrain, stay with the patient; status epilepticus (over 5 minutes) gets lorazepam or diazepam; never stop antiepileptics abruptly. GBS: watch gag and corneal reflexes and vital signs; it can need a ventilator fast.
Stroke & NIRGet the time of onset; tPA within 3 to 4.5 hours, never with blood on the CT. Watch for worsening in the first 24 to 48 hours. NPO until the swallow evaluation. Don't pull on the affected arm; no more than 30 minutes on the affected side. Speak slowly, don't shout. Thunderclap headache means subarachnoid hemorrhage. Frequent NIH Stroke Scale checks and access-site checks after NIR.
