The Lectures' Own Order

Week 4 starts with how the endocrine system works (glands, hormones, negative feedback), then takes each gland and asks the same question: what happens with too much, and what happens with not enough?

StepWhere It LivesThe Common Thread
How It WorksTopic 2, Endocrine Labs & DxThe pituitary is the master gland; the HPA axis and the thermostat-style negative feedback loop; ADH moves pure water; T3 and T4; the parathyroids and calcium.

High-Alert Safety Rules — All in One Place

The danger callouts and safety statements from across the five pages, condensed. Click through to a topic for the full explanation.

TopicThe Hard Rules
ADH DisordersCorrect sodium slowly: hypertonic saline too fast can be fatal. Confused patients are at risk for injury. In DI, rising urine volume with falling specific gravity means it's getting worse: notify the provider.
Adrenal DisordersCushing's: infection, skin tears, fractures, hypokalemia, unstable glucose; taper steroids only under the provider. Addison's: never stop replacement abruptly; 3 times the dose for 3 days when stressed; emergency kit and medic alert bracelet; Addisonian crisis needs IV cortisol, saline and dextrose and close vital signs.
Thyroid DisordersAfter a thyroidectomy: watch for hemorrhage (look behind the neck) and tracheal compression; semi-Fowler's; watch for hypocalcemia. Radioactive iodine: limit contact 2 to 3 days, double flush. Thyroid storm and myxedema coma are emergencies.
DMNever give insulin without knowing the blood glucose; check intake and NPO status. Hypoglycemia below 70: rule of 15 if awake and able to swallow; glucagon or IV dextrose if not. Keep taking medications on sick days. Don't exercise below 80 or above 250.
