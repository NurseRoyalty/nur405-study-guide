The Negative Feedback System

The endocrine system works on a negative feedback system that creates homeostasis, like the thermostat in your living room: set it at 72, the temperature drops below 72, the heater kicks on until it reaches 72, then shuts off. "An important concept to understand with this content": every hormone works this way.

StepThe HPA Axis (Hypothalamus, Pituitary, Adrenal Cortex)
1. StressorA test, a move, a wedding, illness, major surgery.
2. HypothalamusReleases CRF (corticotropin releasing factor). Target: the anterior pituitary.
3. Anterior pituitarySecretes ACTH (adrenocorticotropic hormone). Target: the adrenal cortex.
4. Adrenal cortexReleases cortisol into the blood (measurable with a lab draw).
5. EffectCortisol increases blood sugar for fight or flight: why stress drives blood sugar up.

ADH: The Regulator of Pure Water

  • ADH (antidiuretic hormone, also called vasopressin) comes from the posterior pituitary. It is "the regulator of pure water supply" ("write that down").
  • The loop: serum osmolality rises (concentrated blood), ADH is released, the kidney tubules reabsorb water, plasma volume rises and osmolality falls, and the normal osmolality then inhibits ADH.
ADH moves pure water, not potassium. Retaining water from ADH does not retain potassium; losing water from too little ADH (diabetes insipidus) does not make the patient hypokalemic. It's not like diuresing with Lasix. "That's going to trip you up if you don't stop and understand this."

Thyroid and Parathyroid

Thyroid

  • Regulated by TSH from the anterior pituitary.
  • Secretes T3 and T4: T4 is the most abundant; T3 is the most potent.
  • Also calcitonin (not discussed much in this course).

Parathyroids

  • A different gland, buried inside the thyroid; regulate calcium and phosphorus.
  • If they're inadvertently removed during a thyroidectomy, the patient can become hypocalcemic (Chvostek's and Trousseau's signs).

Adrenal Gland and Pancreas

Adrenal gland

  • Two glands in one: the cortex covers (outside) and the medulla is in the middle.
  • They do totally different things (see Adrenal Disorders).

Pancreas

  • The islets of Langerhans secrete insulin.
  • In diabetes mellitus, the origin of the problem is the pancreas.
