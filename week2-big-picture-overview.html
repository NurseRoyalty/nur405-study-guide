The Lectures' Own Order

Week 2 moves through three body areas, each in the same pattern: the labs and diagnostics first, then the disorders.

StepWhere It LivesThe Common Thread
The EyeTopic 2, Eye DisordersTests (Snellen, ophthalmoscopy, glare test, slit lamp, tonometry, gonioscopy), then cataracts, retinopathy, retinal detachment, AMD and glaucoma. Intraocular pressure runs through almost all of it.
The ProstateTopic 3, ProstateDRE, PSA, TRUS and biopsy, then BPH (benign, with TURP and CBI) and prostate cancer (slow-growing, screening is a shared decision). BPH does not predispose to prostate cancer.
Sexually Transmitted InfectionsTopic 4, STDsSix infections: three bacterial and reportable (chlamydia, gonorrhea, syphilis), one protozoan (trichomoniasis), two viral (herpes, HPV). Treat partners, abstain, retest.

The Three Topics at a Glance

#TopicOne-Line Takeaway
2Eye DisordersNormal IOP is 10 to 21 mmHg. Cataracts are constant fuzzy vision with glare; retinopathy is splotchy; retinal detachment is a painless curtain and urgent; AMD takes central vision; glaucoma takes peripheral vision, and acute angle closure is an emergency. Mydriatics dilate, miotics constrict.
3ProstatePSA is specific to the prostate, not to cancer; only TRUS with biopsy diagnoses cancer. BPH gives irritative and obstructive symptoms (nocturia first); TURP returns with a three-way catheter and CBI titrated to light pink urine; true urine = drained minus irrigation.
4STDsGonorrhea, chlamydia and syphilis are reportable; the male condom is the best protection. Chlamydia and gonorrhea can cause PID; trichomoniasis can't. Herpes is lifelong (acyclovir); removing warts doesn't clear HPV; syphilis is treated with penicillin G.

High-Alert Safety Rules — All in One Place

The danger callouts and safety statements from across the three pages, condensed. Click through to a topic for the full explanation.

TopicThe Hard Rules
Eye DisordersRetinal detachment is urgent: sudden floaters or flashes, then a painless curtain. Acute angle-closure glaucoma is an ocular emergency: treat pain and nausea aggressively, give miotics before darkening the room. Never give dilating drops with closed-angle glaucoma. After eye surgery, avoid anything that raises IOP (bending at the waist, straining, lifting, coughing).
ProstateA prostate large enough that the patient cannot urinate is an emergency. Avoid decongestants and antihistamines with BPH. After TURP, titrate CBI to light pink urine; a large amount of bright red blood may mean hemorrhage, and worsening color for no apparent reason means notify the provider.
STDsGonorrhea, chlamydia and syphilis must be reported to the health department. Treat partners, abstain at least 7 days after treatment, and retest. Hormonal birth control and IUDs do not prevent STIs. Be warm and non-judgmental.
